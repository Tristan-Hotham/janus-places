## Summary of the contribution (150 words)

Studies of constituency focus measure how far MPs speak for their constituencies inside Parliament. This paper puts that measure beside a second channel MPs control: their Facebook pages. For 358 MP-seat spells from February 2017 to October 2020, I fixed in advance a list of places in each seat and counted, with the same matching rule, how often each MP's posts and spoken Commons contributions named them. MPs named their own seat's places 42 times per 100 posts and 9 times per 100 contributions. Within MP and month, that is a rate ratio of 4.4 (95% CI 3.8 to 5.1), and 315 of 331 MPs had a higher rate on Facebook. A placebo (places in other seats) shows no gap, and fifteen checks agree. In frontbench months the Commons rate is 77% lower, while the Facebook rate is unchanged. Marginal seats have a smaller gap, not a larger one. The paper extends Fenno's home style and the Janus-faced campaigning concept to individual MPs and places.
